## Key takeaways

- Survey design requires careful consideration of relevance, accuracy, and respondent burden
- Data collection methods should align with research questions and consider potential bias
- Critical multiplism involves using multiple methods to strengthen observational accuracy
- Survey response rates and representativeness significantly impact data validity
- The data science project process includes problem framing, data understanding, measurement, exploration, modeling, and evaluation
- Ethical considerations are important when collecting and interpreting data
## Discussed topics

### Survey design and response rates

Professor Daniel led a discussion about survey design challenges and considerations.
- **Details**
    - **Clark**: Questioned the validity of survey data with low response rates, citing an example of 135 responses from a population of 95,000
    - **Pitts**: Discussed the balance between survey length and frequency, noting how respondents abandon surveys when they appear too long
    - **Charlotte**: Highlighted challenges in getting representative survey responses, especially from parents in educational settings
    - **E.J.**: Expressed concern about uncertainty in survey design and interpretation
    - **Catherine**: Noted how the absence of data can itself be meaningful data
    - **Cynthia**: Emphasized that "a question is a promise" - asking questions creates an expectation that the data will be used meaningfully
- **Conclusion**
    - Survey design should balance comprehensiveness with respondent burden
    - Low response rates raise questions about data validity and representativeness
    - How data is used and communicated back to participants affects trust and future participation
### Critical multiplism vs. triangulation

The class discussed the distinction between critical multiplism and triangulation in research.
- **Details**
    - **Kylie**: Asked about when multiple data sources constitute critical multiplism versus simply collecting different types of data
    - **Daniel**: Explained that triangulation is using multiple methods to collect data, while critical multiplism is intentionally introducing different methods to strengthen observational accuracy
- **Conclusion**
    - Triangulation is one element of critical multiplism but doesn't necessarily mean critical multiplism is being applied
    - Critical multiplism involves intentionally introducing variance in methods to strengthen validity
