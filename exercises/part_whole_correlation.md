# Average Item–Total (Part–Whole) Correlation in XLMiner

## Understanding the Concept
- **Item–Total Correlation**: Correlation between each item’s scores and the **total score of the test**.
- **Part–Whole Correction**: The total score **excludes** the item being correlated (to avoid artificially inflating the correlation).
- **Average Item–Total Correlation**: The mean of all part–whole correlations across items.

---

## Steps in XLMiner

### 1. Prepare Your Data
Arrange your dataset in Excel:

| Respondent | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 |
|------------|----|----|----|----|
| 1          | 4  | 3  | 5  | 2  |
| 2          | 5  | 4  | 4  | 3  |
| 3          | 3  | 2  | 4  | 1  |

- **Rows** = respondents  
- **Columns** = items (e.g., Q1, Q2, Q3…)  
- Each cell = numeric score for that item.

---

### 2. Compute Part–Whole Totals
For each item:

1. Create a **"Total without this item"** column.  
   Example for Q1:

    ```excel
    =SUM($B2:$E2) - B2
    ```

2. Repeat for each item.

---

### 3. Run Correlation in XLMiner
1. Go to **XLMiner → Data Analysis → Correlation**.
2. Select:
   - **Input Range**: The column for the item and its corresponding "Total without this item".
   - **Correlation Method**: Pearson.
3. Click **OK** to get the correlation coefficient.
4. Record the correlation for each item.

---

### 4. Calculate the Average
Once you have all part–whole correlations, use:

```excel
=AVERAGE(range_of_correlations)
```
##This gives the **Average Item–Total (Part–Whole) Correlation**.

## Tips

- Ensure all items are coded in the same direction (reverse-score if needed).
- Use Pearson correlation for continuous/interval data.
- If you have many items, you can automate the process with Excel formulas before running XLMiner.
